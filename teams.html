How much AI assistance can your codebase actually withstand?

If your team is generating code with Claude, Cursor, Copilot, or any other AI assistant, the codebase is changing faster than your review can catch. PragmaWorks audits what's drifting, scores what your team can measure today, and remediates what needs to be fixed — so AI assistance compounds your engineering, instead of slowly eroding it.

The four things every CTO and VP Engineering wants to know

Existing tools (SonarQube, CodeClimate, Snyk) measure code metrics. PragmaWorks measures the metrics that determine whether AI assistance helps or hurts your organization over time:

Drift from intent.

How far has your codebase moved from its architectural intent in the last quarter? AI sessions don't preserve invariants the way humans do. We measure structural drift commit by commit.

AI-introduced bug rate.

What percentage of bugs introduced in the last 90 days came from AI-assisted commits? We detect the signal in commit history — message patterns, branch naming, change size, time of day — and report it.

Team habit health.

PR review density, regression coverage of past bugs, collaboration distribution across members, commit-size distribution. The organizational metrics no static analyzer surfaces.

AI readiness score.

0-14 across seven properties: Self-describing, Bounded, Composable, Verifiable, Auditable, Defended, Executable. Each scored with evidence and a specific path to improve. Reproducible across runs, defensible at the next CTO meeting.

What you get

An HTML and PDF report, on a branch, never touching your main. Eight sections, designed to be forwarded by the developer who ran it to the manager who didn't:

  • Cover summary — one-page AI readiness score with overall grade and top three risks
  • Structural disciplines — SOLID, TDD, hexagonal/clean architecture adherence
  • Documentation health — existence, staleness, alignment to current code
  • Test pyramid coverage — unit, integration, e2e — gaps and skew
  • Seven GS rubric scores — each with "why this score" and "how to improve"
  • Security and logging baseline — gaps, sensitive data handling, observability
  • Team-habit analysis — commit history patterns, AI bug rate, collaboration graph
  • Roadmap to remediation — prioritized action list, scoped to your architecture

How it works

01
Audit. A developer or tech lead runs one command on any repo. The audit uses your existing AI assistant — Claude Code, Cursor, Copilot — and produces the report on a new branch. No infrastructure changes, no code changes to main.
npx pragmaworks audit
02
Remediate. The same flow runs the AI through a prioritized remediation under specification governance — generating use cases, ADRs, the test harness, the deployment scaffolding, and the corrected code. All on the branch. You review, you merge what passes.
npx pragmaworks remediate
03
Govern. The team subscription adds multi-repo dashboards, ongoing drift monitoring, Chronicle Team for shared organizational memory, KPI tracking across the org, and licensing for commercial use.
